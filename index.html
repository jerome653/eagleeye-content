Local map rank tracking, point by point

One rank for “pizza near me” is true from exactly one spot: your desk. On Google Maps and Apple Maps the top three change with every block the searcher is standing on, so a single number is a figure you can’t defend to a client. Eagle Eye is a geo-grid rank tracker: it lays a grid of up to 441 points over a business’s neighbourhood and runs a real map search from every one. You get the rank street by street — where it wins, where it drops off the map, and which rival is taking its customers there.

Every scan folds the whole grid into three numbers a client actually remembers — LSOV, ARP and ATRP — maps every competitor that outranks you point by point, and keeps every run, so the trend builds itself. Questions, answered

What is geo-grid rank tracking?

Instead of one rank for a keyword, Eagle Eye lays a grid of up to 441 points over a business’s neighbourhood and runs a real Google Maps or Apple Maps search from each one. You get the rank street by street, because the local pack reorders with every block the searcher is standing on.

How is Eagle Eye different from a normal rank checker?

A normal rank checker gives one position from one spot. Eagle Eye gives a whole map: where a business wins the top 3, where it drops off, and which rival is taking its customers there — folded into LSOV, ARP and ATRP.

Does it track both Google Maps and Apple Maps?

Yes. Eagle Eye runs the same geo-grid on Google Maps and on Apple Maps at the same price. Each scan reads one map, so run both and you get the two side by side — including the Apple picture most tools never measure.

What does a scan cost?

Every plan is a bucket of credits and a scan spends one credit per grid point: 81 credits for a 9 × 9 scan, up to 441 for a 21 × 21. Points you switch off or leave outside your shape cost nothing, and credits you buy never expire.

Can I try it free?

A new organisation starts with 10 free scans, and your first scan of your own website is free at any size up to 21 × 21 — no card needed.
